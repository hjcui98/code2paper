## Motivation: revisit GraphRAG shortcomings from relation extraction errors

The intended motivation for this work is to revisit the shortcomings of GraphRAG that arise from errors in relation extraction: inaccuracies in the extracted relations propagate through the knowledge graph and degrade downstream retrieval quality, motivating a design that replaces explicit relation extraction with a graph-structured retrieval that does not require pre-extracted relations.

## Offline Tri‑Graph construction

The offline construction phase instantiates the retrieval system with a dedicated NER component that extracts named entities from each sentence in the corpus. During initialization, the system assigns a compute device and constructs a `SpacyNER` instance from the configured spaCy model, which is stored as the entity-extraction backend. The resulting graph contains three node types—entities, sentences, and passages—linked by sparse adjacency matrices that encode occurrence relationships only; no relation triples are introduced (`not sentence_hash_ids` excludes entities lacking valid sentence associations from the graph structure). At query time, the retrieval method computes the shape of the entity-to-sentence sparse matrix to determine the effective edge counts for downstream propagation.

At query time, the retrieval pipeline loads the configuration flag `self.config.use_vectorized_retrieval` to determine whether to precompute sparse adjacency matrices for batch propagation. When the vectorized path is selected (`self.config.use_vectorized_retrieval`), the system precomputes the sentence–entity and entity–sentence sparse matrices to enable efficient batch operations. Entity scores are computed by propagating activation through the sentence–entity subgraph: for each candidate entity, the score is derived from the top-scoring sentence that mentions it, and values that fail the comparison `len(entity_to_sentence_indices) > 0` are excluded from further consideration. The retrieval method returns a ranked list of passage indices and their associated scores as the final output.

Passage scores are computed by a hybrid formula that combines a dense-retrieval similarity component with an entity-based bonus. The dense-retrieval passage score is multiplied by a configurable ratio (`self.config.passage_ratio`), and this product is added to the natural logarithm of one plus the total entity bonus, where the entity bonus accumulates per-entity contributions weighted by occurrence count. The resulting composite score is then scaled by a configurable passage-node weight (`self.config.passage_node_weight`) to yield the final passage weight. Entity scores are filtered so that values that fail the comparison `not sentence_hash_ids` are excluded, ensuring that only entities with valid sentence associations contribute to the bonus. The sparse-matrix precomputation step guards against empty adjacency structures by branching on the condition `len(sentence_to_entity_indices) > 0`.

The passage weight vector is formed by scaling each passage's composite score by the configurable passage-node weight, and the entity weight vector is derived from the propagated entity scores. These two vectors are summed element-wise to produce the initial node-weight vector that seeds the personalized PageRank computation. Dense passage retrieval produces a ranked list of passage indices and their similarity scores against the query embedding, which feeds the dense-retrieval component of the hybrid formula. The passage-node weight parameter (`self.config.passage_ratio`) controls the relative contribution of the dense-retrieval similarity signal versus the entity-based evidence in the final ranking.

The personalized PageRank routine iterates over all vertices in the graph, indexed by the node-name-to-vertex-index mapping, and produces a sorted list of passage indices together with their PPR scores. The non-zero counts of the entity-to-sentence and sentence-to-entity sparse matrices are queried to determine the effective edge counts for the propagation. The PPR-sorted passage scores are written to the output buffer as the final ranked passage list. The vertex iteration range is determined by the length of the node-name-to-vertex-index mapping, ensuring that all graph nodes participate in the importance aggregation.

The node-weight vector that initializes the personalized PageRank is the element-wise sum of the entity weight vector and the passage weight vector. The entity weights are derived from the propagated entity scores after threshold-based filtering, where values that fail the iteration threshold comparison are excluded. The passage weights encode the hybrid score described above. The PPR personalization distribution is set from this combined node-weight vector, and the routine propagates influence across the full vertex set to produce a globally ranked passage list. The configuration parameters `passage_node_weight` and `passage_ratio` jointly determine the relative weighting of entity-based and similarity-based evidence in the final ranking. The offline construction and the two-stage retrieval mechanism together form a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline that operates over entity-based graph indexing without requiring relation extraction. The hierarchical graph structure preserves original text at the passage and sentence levels alongside extracted entities, and the two-stage process—local entity activation followed by global importance aggregation—enables multi-hop query resolution over the corpus.

The offline Tri-Graph construction and the subsequent two-stage retrieval mechanism together constitute the core indexing and ranking pipeline. The hierarchical graph preserves the original text hierarchy—passages containing sentences containing entities—alongside the extracted entity layer, ensuring that textual context is retained at every level of the graph structure.

## First retrieval stage: relevant entity activation via local semantic bridging

The first retrieval stage activates relevant entities through iterative breadth-first frontier propagation over the sentence–entity subgraph. The propagation state is initialized by loading the iteration threshold `iteration_threshold` from the configuration, which governs both the branching condition and the pruning criterion. At each iteration, the frontier of `current_entities` is expanded: entities whose score satisfies (`entity_score < self.config.iteration_threshold`) are retained in the active frontier, while those at or above the threshold are pruned. The vectorized propagation step computes next-entity scores by constructing a mask over scores that meet or exceed the threshold (`next_entity_scores_np >= self.config.iteration_threshold`), yielding the set of active indices for the next frontier. Propagation continues while (`len(current_entities) > 0 and iteration < self.config.max_iterations`), ensuring that the frontier terminates when no qualifying entities remain or the maximum iteration budget is exhausted. The iterative propagation step is formalized as follows. Given the current entity score $s_{e}^{(t)}$ at iteration $t$, the next score is updated by taking the maximum over all sentences $s$ in the neighborhood $N(e)$ of entity $e$ of the product of the current score and the query–sentence similarity $sim(q, s)$: s_{e}^{(t+1)} = max_{s in N(e)} ( s_{e}^{(t)} cdot sim(q, s) ) Here $s_{e}^{(t)}$ denotes the relevance score assigned to entity $e$ at iteration $t$, $N(e)$ is the set of sentence nodes adjacent to $e$ in the subgraph, and $sim(q, s)$ is the similarity between query $q$ and sentence $s$. The reset-probability vector is derived from node weights by zeroing entries that are NaN or negative, and the sentence-activation vector is computed as the sparse matrix product of the transposed entity-to-sentence incidence matrix and the current score vector. A top-$k$ selection over the resulting sentence similarities yields the locally active sentences that seed the next propagation round.

The first retrieval stage couples initialization of the entity frontier, iterative score propagation through the structural sentence–entity topology, and dynamic pruning of low-scoring entities into a single deterministic pipeline. The propagation mechanism identifies latent intermediate entities that support multi-hop connections by iteratively expanding the frontier based on query–sentence similarity, without requiring a dedicated relation-extraction module. This design trades relational expressiveness for deterministic construction and robustness, recovering retrieval quality through score propagation through the structural topology rather than through edge typing.

Entity activation via semantic bridging propagates relevance through the sentence–entity subgraph based on query–sentence similarity, identifying latent intermediate entities that support multi-hop connections. This mechanism is intended to avoid explicit relation extraction entirely; the graph topology is derived from structural co-occurrence rather than from learned or rule-based relation labels, trading relational expressiveness for deterministic construction and robustness.

## Second retrieval stage: passage retrieval via global importance aggregation

The retrieval pipeline is organized into two decoupled stages: a local entity-activation stage that propagates scores through the entity–sentence subgraph, and a global passage-ranking stage that aggregates those scores to the passage level. The second stage operates on a passage–entity graph constructed as an undirected graph (`directed=False`) during initialization, where the retrieval method is selected and stored as a configuration field. Edges between passage and entity nodes are appended as `(node_hash_id, neighbor_hash_id)` pairs, and self-loops are excluded by the filter condition `node_hash_id == neighbor_hash_id`. The indexing procedure branches on the condition `len(new_passage_hash_ids) > 0` to determine whether new passages require graph updates before retrieval proceeds.

Personalized PageRank propagates importance scores across the undirected passage–entity graph using the `prpack` implementation, with a damping factor and reset probability read from the configuration. Node weights that are NaN or negative are masked out before the ranking computation. The resulting document scores are sorted in descending order via `np.argsort`, and the method returns the sorted passage hash identifiers together with their corresponding passage scores as a list.

The decoupling of local activation from global ranking permits independent tuning: the local stage controls how far score propagation reaches through the entity–sentence subgraph, while the global stage governs how sentence-level evidence is combined into passage-level evidence.

The exact PPR parameters—damping factor, number of iterations, and convergence criterion—are implementation details pending author confirmation; the boundary between the local and global stages (i.e., whether the walk traverses the full graph or a pruned subgraph) likewise requires author confirmation.
