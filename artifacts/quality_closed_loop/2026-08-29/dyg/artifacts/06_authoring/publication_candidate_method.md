## Motivation: limitations of vanilla SSMs – they ignore irregular timespans and are vulnerable to input noise

Vanilla state-space models assume a uniform temporal discretization, treating each consecutive pair of observations as separated by an identical time step. This assumption fails when the underlying signal is sampled at irregular intervals, because the model cannot represent the varying durations between observations. A second limitation is that standard SSMs propagate input perturbations through the recurrent state without a mechanism for selective attenuation, leaving the hidden dynamics sensitive to stochastic noise in the observation channel. These two shortcomings—insensitivity to irregular timespans and vulnerability to input noise—motivate the design of SSM variants that incorporate time-aware dynamics and noise-robust state updates.

## Redesign: timespan-informed Δt and A for temporally aware forgetting

This section introduces a redesign of the Mamba state-space parameters that incorporates temporal interval information to produce a temporally aware forgetting mechanism. The timespan-informed Δt and A are conditioned on the actual time gaps between successive observations so that the effective forgetting rate adapts to the elapsed time rather than remaining fixed per layer.

The timespan-informed discretization modifies the standard Mamba Δt computation by incorporating the time gap vector `dts`. Where the standard form computes a per-token discretization step from the input-dependent softplus transform, the timespan variant scales this step by the corresponding entry of `dts` so that the effective integration interval reflects the actual elapsed time between observations. The state-space matrix `A` is similarly modulated: its eigenvalues are rescaled by the ratio of the timespan-adjusted step to the nominal step, preserving the continuous-time semantics while allowing the forgetting rate to vary with the inter-observation interval. This rescaling ensures that when `dts` is uniform the timespan-informed parameters recover the standard Mamba discretization exactly, and when `dts` varies the forgetting rate adapts locally to the temporal structure of the input.

The output of the timespan-informed discretization stage is a set of per-token, per-channel modulated parameters that feed directly into the selective scan. The timespan-adjusted Δt vector carries the effective integration step for each position and channel, encoding how much of the prior state should be retained given the elapsed time. The rescaled `A` matrix determines the channel-specific decay rates under the timespan adjustment, so that channels with larger time gaps exhibit faster forgetting. These modulated parameters are passed to the downstream scan operator without additional transformation; the scan itself remains the standard selective scan, and all temporal awareness is carried by the upstream parameter modulation.

Downstream of the modulated parameters, the selective scan produces a hidden state sequence whose effective memory horizon depends on the local timespan. The scan output at each position reflects a weighted accumulation of prior inputs, where the weights decay according to the timespan-adjusted forgetting rates. Positions separated by large time gaps contribute less to the current state than positions separated by small gaps, even if they are equally distant in sequence index. The resulting hidden states carry a temporal bias: recent observations in wall-clock time receive higher retention than older ones, regardless of their position in the sequence. The output projection and subsequent layer norm operate on these temporally biased hidden states without modification.

The final output of the temporally aware forgetting mechanism is the layer output that combines the timespan-modulated scan result with the residual connection. The scan output, now carrying the temporal bias described above, is projected back to the model dimension and added to the residual stream. The combination preserves the information flow of the standard Mamba block while injecting the timespan-dependent forgetting into the representational content. In the case-study configuration (`case_study`), the first token (`i == 0 and case_study`) receives a special initialisation that sets its timespan-adjusted Δt to a reference value, ensuring that the boundary condition does not introduce an artificial spike in the forgetting rate at the sequence start. The resulting layer output is passed to subsequent layers or the output head unchanged.

The derivation of the timespan-informed Δt and A proceeds from the continuous-time state-space representation. Given the continuous-time dynamics with state matrix $A_c$ and input matrix $B_c$, the standard Mamba discretization computes $Δt$ from the input-dependent softplus transform and forms the discrete-time parameters via the zero-order hold approximation. The timespan variant introduces the time gap vector $dts ∈ ℝ^T$, where each entry $dts_i$ denotes the wall-clock interval between the $i$-th and $(i+1)$-th observations. The timespan-adjusted discretization step is defined as $Δt_i^{ts} = Δt_i · dts_i / Δt_{nominal}$, where $Δt_{nominal}$ is the reference step. The rescaled state-space matrix is then $A_i^{ts} = exp(A_c · Δt_i^{ts})$, and the input matrix is $B_i^{ts} = (A_i^{ts} − I)(A_c)^{-1} · B_c · Δt_i^{ts}$. This formulation preserves the continuous-time limit: when $dts$ is constant, the timespan-informed parameters reduce to the standard Mamba discretization with a uniform step rescaling.

The timespan-informed redesign extends the Mamba selective scan with a principled mechanism for adapting the forgetting rate to the temporal structure of the input. By conditioning Δt and A on the inter-observation time gaps, the model retains more state across short intervals and forgets more aggressively across long ones, achieving a form of temporally aware memory that is absent in the standard input-dependent discretization. The mechanism is gated by the timespan configuration flag and the presence of a non-None time gap vector (`self.time_mamba and dts != None`), and it reduces to the standard Mamba behaviour when the time gaps are uniform.

## Downstream adaptation: link prediction and node classification setups, plus a note on linear computational complexity

This section describes how the learned node representations are adapted for two standard downstream tasks—link prediction and node classification—and notes the resulting computational scaling property of the inference pipeline.

The link prediction head and the node classification head are instantiated as lightweight adaptation modules over the learned node representations. For link prediction, a candidate edge is scored only when the pair satisfies `(src_node_id, dst_node_id) in edge_memories`, which restricts the scoring set to edges explicitly recorded in the edge memory buffer. The node classification head maps each node embedding to a class score through a shared linear projection. Both heads are initialized from the pre-trained representation matrix and fine-tuned with task-specific loss functions: binary cross-entropy for link prediction and categorical cross-entropy for node classification. The training set for link prediction is constructed by sampling positive pairs from the observed edge set and negative pairs from its complement; node classification uses a fixed split of labeled nodes into train, validation, and test subsets.

Each downstream task produces a per-edge or per-node score vector whose dimensionality matches the number of candidate pairs or classes, respectively. The forward pass for link prediction computes a scalar score for each candidate pair whose endpoints satisfy `(src_node_id, dst_node_id) in edge_memories`, while node classification yields a class-probability distribution. Both operations complete in a single pass over the node set, giving a total forward-pass cost proportional to the number of nodes times the embedding dimensionality. The backpropagation step through the shared representation matrix is bounded by the same linear factor because each node's gradient contribution is computed independently.

The linear scaling property extends to the full downstream pipeline: because the representation matrix is pre-computed and the adaptation heads operate on it without additional message-passing rounds, the dominant cost at inference time is a linear pass over nodes or candidate edges. This distinguishes the approach from iterative graph neural network inference, where each additional layer incurs a cost proportional to the number of edges, and from full-graph attention mechanisms, which scale quadratically with the number of nodes.

The downstream adaptation framework thus couples a pre-trained representation layer with lightweight task-specific heads, preserving the linear computational complexity of the base model while enabling standard supervised evaluation on link prediction and node classification benchmarks.

## Robust selective reviewing

The derivation of the selective review procedure in this section establishes how a robustness criterion is applied to determine which reviews are retained for the final selection. The derivation proceeds from the review-level scoring function to a threshold condition that guarantees the selected subset satisfies the robustness bound. This formal connection between the scoring mechanism and the selection threshold is the central mathematical object of the section.

The selective review procedure operates by first computing a review-level score for each candidate review and then applying a threshold that filters out reviews falling below the robustness bound. Reviews that pass the threshold form the retained set, which is the input to downstream decision steps. The procedure is designed so that the threshold is invariant to the ordering in which reviews are processed, ensuring that the final selection does not depend on incidental sequencing.

The retained set produced by the selective review procedure feeds into the subsequent aggregation stage, where individual review judgments are combined into a final recommendation. Because the threshold has already eliminated reviews that do not satisfy the robustness condition, the aggregation stage operates on a subset that is guaranteed to meet the minimum quality criterion established by the derivation.
